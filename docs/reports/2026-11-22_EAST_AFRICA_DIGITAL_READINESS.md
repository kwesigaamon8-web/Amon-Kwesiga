## Primary evidence — World Bank World Development Indicators (WDI)
Source: [World Bank comparative country data: Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania](https://data.worldbank.org/?locations=KE-UG-TZ), retrieved 22 November 2026; indicators IT.NET.USER.ZS (internet use) and EG.ELC.ACCS.ZS (electricity access). The WDI dataset [catalog metadata](https://datacatalog.worldbank.org/infrastructure-data/search/dataset/0037712/world-development-indicators) was updated 18 November 2026. The World Bank page gives the **2024** estimates:

| Country | Individuals using the internet, % population, 2024 | Access to electricity, % population, 2024 | Internet-use percentage-point difference vs Kenya |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Kenya | 35 | 77.0 | 0 |
| Uganda | 9 | 55.3 | -26 |
| Tanzania | 31 | 52.4 | -4 |

The last column is an **original derived calculation** (country internet rate minus 35). Kenya's internet-use share in this published indicator is about **3.9 times** Uganda's (35/9, rounded), not necessarily 3.9 times the number of internet users. This rate comparison says nothing about population-weighted market size, quality of service, affordability, smartphone subscriptions or willingness to pay.

## What can a buyer do with this?
1. **A Uganda-focused business should test low-bandwidth and offline-assisted buyer journeys** instead of assuming a nationwide web-only conversion funnel reaches all adults. This is a design hypothesis based on published aggregate figures, not a quantified sales forecast.
2. **A Kenya–Tanzania pilot may test two different online conversion channels** because the internet-use shares are closer (35% vs 31%); account for income, cities, device type and age before extrapolating.
3. **A regional software supplier should test power-interruption tolerance and local caching**; reported national access to electricity is 77.0% (Kenya), 55.3% (Uganda) and 52.4% (Tanzania). National access does not by itself measure reliability.
