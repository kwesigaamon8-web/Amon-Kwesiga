## Why Jupiter (JUP on Solana)?

JUP is an established Solana token with real exchange-product activity and a potentially interesting **market-value-versus-token-value-accrual puzzle**. Jupiter's token buybacks do not automatically confer cash-flow rights on all holders. A critical question is how the token's **tradable float, net supply changes and treasury accounting** interact with operating revenue and buyback disclosures.

Before inferring positive price impact from either number, can you identify the exact time window, buyback transaction set, USD pricing method, treasury-held-versus-burned treatment, and circulating-supply denominator behind your report's number? ## Follow-up questions, each anchored to a concrete live report section

**B — Economic rights vs sentiment.** If REX groups Jupiter product fees/revenue together, which specific revenue sources are genuinely committed to buying JUP, which fund operations/treasury, and which have no direct holder claim? Can REX cite current program terms and avoid treating protocol revenue as a dividend?

**C — Counterfactual scenarios.** Relative to the report's base-case 30-day revenue and JUP repurchase inputs, model (i) revenue -40%, (ii) revenue flat but rising float, and (iii) rising revenue with slippage/market depth falling. Which assumptions dominate buyback yield, *without* inventing certainty about price direction?

**D — Governance timing.** Does the report use an unlock calendar that predates February 2026 "Net-Zero" changes? How would conclusions differ between the historically promised schedules and a snapshot based on currently paused or postponed allocations? Name the future vote or event that would invalidate each scenario.

**E — Market microstructure.** If the report sees high volumes and weakening price performance, what observable venue-level and order-book evidence distinguishes buyback-driven absorption from organic demand or concentrated tokenholder distribution? Give measurable thresholds based on cited historical distributions, not arbitrary heuristics.

**F — Custody versus destruction.** A purchased token can remain in a treasury or be burned. Can REX trace which wallets contain repurchased JUP, whether supply was actually extinguished, and how this distinction changes a "deflationary" narrative?

**G — Concentration risk.** Compare median daily liquidity against the report's estimated treasury repurchase schedule and top-wallet outflows. How sensitive is price impact to a single large seller? Which cited data would reveal spoofed/deceptive liquidity?

**H — Information provenance.** Show the onchain evidence and the primary source used for each major input. If two dashboards disagree, rank the competing explanations (different as-of timestamp, product inclusion, window choice, wallet attribution, USD pricing, annualization). What remains unknowable from public data?

**I — Falsifiable conclusion.** What three observations during the next month would make you *withdraw* the report's strongest bullish or bearish inference? Specify each data field, where to find it and the exact condition, but do not give a buy/sell recommendation.
