## Eight X posts to publish as ONE thread

### Post 1

Your crypto wallet is a keyring, not an undo button. 🔑 I reviewed @KriptoK's public self-custody guides (not a live trading test). Here are six checks worth making before you move coins. @KriptoKLeague

### Post 2

1/6 Who holds the keys? KriptoK says private keys remain on the user's device. That changes *who can authorize* a transfer, but it doesn't eliminate fraud, volatility or user mistakes.

### Post 3

2/6 The recovery phrase is the spare key. Keep it offline and private. If anyone asks you to paste it into a chat, form or support ticket, stop. A real support team should never need your seed phrase.

### Post 4

3/6 Match the *network*, not just the ticker. USDC or another asset can exist on several blockchains. Before receiving or sending, confirm the exact chain, token and destination. A wrong-network transfer may not be recoverable.

### Post 5

4/6 Read the fee screen. KriptoK's public help pages say swaps/perps have trading fees and on-chain transactions can require network gas. A self-custody wallet does not make those charges disappear.

### Post 6

5/6 Know what is risky. KriptoK lists swaps, tokenized stocks, DeFi vaults and perpetuals. Those are different products with different risks; especially for leveraged perps, a price move can liquidate a position.

### Post 7

6/6 Test your knowledge before any trade. Can you identify the official site, explain how recovery works, and describe the fees? KriptoK's site has a self-custody FAQ. Reading it is free; no deposit or transaction is needed.

### Post 8

My takeaway: control is a responsibility, not a promise of profit. Sources: https://www.kriptok.io/ and https://www.kriptok.io/resources. Which check trips people up most—recovery, network selection, or fees? @KriptoK @KriptoKLeague
